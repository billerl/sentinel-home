Help

Getting around
  • Live — all cameras. Tap one for full screen; tap again to go back.
  • Zones: on / off (top of the live page) — shows or hides the zone outlines on the live view. Detection still uses the zones either way. It applies to everyone watching.
  • Clock — the weather card on the live page shows the time and date, as big as the temperature, so it can be read across the room. 🌅 and 🌇 under it are today's sunrise and sunset.
  • Data saver: on / off (top of the live page) — for phone hotspots and mobile data. The grid uses small pictures refreshed every ~5 seconds (about 0.1 GB an hour instead of 0.4–1.3 GB), and full screen shows one sharp picture a second instead of live video. It's remembered per device, so turning it on on your phone doesn't affect your computer.
  • Asking Claude — if “ask Claude” is on under Telegram, any message Sentinel doesn't understand (“was it busy out back this afternoon?”, “did Liz leave before or after Kate got home?”) is answered by Claude from the last 2 days of events. You'll see “🤔 Let me think…” first; answers take about 10 seconds. It uses your Claude subscription, up to the daily limit you set.
  • Talking to Sentinel (“Jezebel”, or “Hey Jarvis” if that's the wake word in use) — if the voice assistant is on under Audio, say “Hey Jarvis”, wait for the rising chime, then ask: “who's home?”, “when did Liz get home?”, “did anyone come by today?”, “what's the weather?”, “what time is it?”. It answers out loud. Anything else goes to Claude (“Let me think…”) if that's on. To change a setting (“enable messages”) it asks first and only does it if you say “yes”. After each answer it chimes again and listens for about 5 seconds, so you can ask another question without “Hey Jarvis”. If what it heard made no sense (“Huh?”), it stops listening right away; so does a “Stop.” in the middle of other talk. A falling chime means it has stopped listening; say “Hey Jarvis” to start again. Wake it with “Hey Jarvis” and Jarvis answers in a man's voice (chosen under Audio); with “Jezebel”, Jezebel answers in the main voice. If it starts listening by mistake, say “cancel” or “never mind” (even at the end of whatever it overheard) and it stops without answering. Speak toward the microphone; from across the room, speak up a little. If it wakes up by itself (the TV, people talking), raise Wake word: how sure it must be under Audio; if it misses you, lower it.
  • Reminders — say “Jezebel, remind me Wednesday at 9 am about my PT appointment”. She reads the day and time back and saves it only when you say “yes”; at that time Sentinel sends you the reminder on Telegram. “What are my reminders?” lists them. (Needs “Ask Claude”, because Claude works out the date.)
  • Smart plugs and switches — The Switches tile on the Home page (or 💡 Switches at the top of the live page; on a phone, the Switches tab at the bottom) shows every Kasa smart plug, outlet and wall switch on your Wi-Fi. Tap a tile to turn it on or off; lit tiles glow and say how long they've been on. A plug with two outlets shows a tile for each. The names are the ones from the Kasa app; ✎ renames one (the Kasa app gets the new name too). By voice: “Hey Jarvis, turn on the patio lights”, “patio lights off”, “are the patio lights on?”, or just “lights on” for every light. Set a new plug up in the Kasa app first (it joins your Wi-Fi there), then tap Find new devices. The Remotes tile opens a TV's remote straight away (with more than one TV, pick which at the top of the remote). On a Fire TV the remote also shows what the TV is showing, live, so you can read its menus from across the room; tap the picture to click there (most menus take taps; the arrow buttons always work). Netflix, Prime Video and other paid streaming apps show black there: they block screen copies. Everything stays on your home network; Sentinel doesn't use the Kasa cloud. Plugs running the free Tasmota firmware (many older Wi-Fi plugs can be switched to it) show up the same way, a few seconds after they join your Wi-Fi.
  • Samsung TV — a smart Samsung TV on your network shows up as a 📺 tile beside the plugs. The first time, the TV shows an “Allow” pop-up for Sentinel: press Allow with the TV remote (if it never appears: TV Settings → General → External Device Manager → Device Connection Manager → Access Notification). By voice or Telegram: “turn on the TV”, “TV off”, “put on Netflix”, “open Disney Plus” or “turn on the TV to YouTube TV” (any app installed on the TV; it turns the TV on first), “TV volume up” / “turn the TV down”, “mute the TV”, “mute the TV for 2 minutes” (the sound comes back by itself), “pause the TV” / “resume the TV”, “rewind 30 seconds” / “fast forward 2 minutes” (in 10-second steps, the way the TV apps skip; a long skip takes a few seconds, then it plays from the new spot), “what's on the TV?”, “show me the front door” (one camera), “show me Kate's location” / “show me where Kate is” / “show me everyone's location” (a map of where they are, zoomed out to fit everyone; each person's picture is their pin: add pictures with 📷 Add picture on the Family page; the TV's Back button returns to the cameras). Afterwards “go back to YouTube” (or “back to the show”) returns to the app that was on, where it was, like the remote's ⏎ Back to … button. To Jarvis, “pause”, “play”, “mute”, “rewind …” and “fast forward …” mean the TV while it's on, even without saying “TV”; say “Spotify” or “the music” (“pause the music”) for Spotify. If you mute or unmute with the TV's own remote during a timed mute, the timer still presses mute once more when it ends. Turning it on needs the TV's Power On with Mobile setting (Settings → General → Network → Expert Settings); it takes about 15 seconds. Sentinel can open an app but not pick a channel or show inside it. 🎮 Open remote on the TV's tile is a full remote: power, arrows (tap them, or swipe on the circle; hold to repeat), OK, Back, Home, Input, volume, channels, play/pause, a number pad, a box that types into the TV's search, and every app with the TV's own icons (icons appear once the TV has been on). On a computer the keyboard works too: arrows, Enter, Backspace for Back, + and − for volume, M to mute. Shortcuts (beta), near the bottom of the remote, record your presses so one tap does them again: tap ⏺ Record a shortcut, then, watching the TV, tap the app first (it always starts from the app's first screen) and press buttons as usual; type a name (“Judge Judy”) and tap ⏹ Save. ▶ plays it back with the same pauses, a little slower. Use the app's search rather than its home rows, which change every day. Only presses made on Sentinel's remote are recorded, not the TV's own remote.
  • Fire TV — a Fire TV with ADB debugging turned on (Settings → My Fire TV → Developer Options) shows up as a 📺 tile with the same remote; the first time, press OK on its “Allow USB debugging?” pop-up (tick “Always allow”). Fire TVs without that setting are left alone. A Fire TV showing Sentinel's live view (Fully Kiosk Browser) gets 📹 Show cameras and ↻ Reload cameras buttons; by voice or Telegram: “put the cameras on the Sentinel TV”, “reload the cameras”, “open Prime Video on the Sentinel TV”. Hold ⌂ Home on its remote for a second to open the Fire TV's quick menu (Picture → Backlight dims the screen; Sleep Timer). Say the TV's name when you have more than one; “the TV” alone means the Samsung.
  • Remotes (BroadLink) — for things that only have a remote, like a heater, a soundbar or ceiling fans. Needs a BroadLink RM4 Pro (infrared and radio) or RM4 mini (infrared only). Set it up once in the BroadLink app: it joins 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only, and in the device's settings turn Lock device off (a locked BroadLink answers only its app). Then open Remotes from the Home page and tap Look for it. To teach a button: type the remote's name (“Soundbar”) and the button's (“Volume up”), choose Infrared (you point the remote at the thing: TVs, soundbars, heaters) or Radio (works through walls: most ceiling fans), tap Learn this button and follow what the page says, holding the remote about 15 cm (6 in) from the BroadLink. Infrared is one press. Radio is two steps: hold the button until the page changes, then press it once more. Then tap the button on the page, or say or send its names: “Hey Jarvis, soundbar volume up 3 times”, “heater power”, “turn the soundbar up”. ✎ Edit renames, re-teaches or deletes buttons. Infrared needs a clear line from the BroadLink to the heater or soundbar; radio goes through walls and floors. If a fan remote won't learn, it may use a radio the BroadLink can't hear (2.4 GHz or Bluetooth). A Power button that turns things both on and off is only pressed when you name it: “turn off the heater” isn't sent to it, because Sentinel can't see whether the heater is on.
  • Heater thermostat — holds a room temperature with a heater that only has a remote, like an EdenPURE in Auto mode. It needs the BroadLink's temperature sensor cable, and the heater's Temp up and Temp down buttons taught on the Remotes page. The 🔥 card there shows the room temperature; set the temperature with − and +, check that the heater is on with its Auto light lit (not High), and tap Start holding. Sentinel first presses Temp down 14 times to find the bottom of the heater's setting, then every 15 minutes moves it one step warmer or cooler toward your temperature, and remembers which step held it (more than 3° short, it goes to the top step until the room is close). By voice or Telegram: “set the heater to 70”, “keep the room at 68”, “how warm is it inside?”, “stop holding the temperature”. It never presses Power or Mode by itself: those buttons switch back and forth, and Sentinel can't see the heater, so a press could turn it on or to High by mistake. Turn it on and off yourself (the Power button on the page works). Safety: at 78°F, or if the room temperature stops coming in, the setting goes all the way down and you get a Telegram message; if the room stays well over your temperature with the setting at the bottom, the heater may be on High (where the Temp buttons do nothing), and Sentinel tells you to check it. Put the BroadLink where the heater can't shine straight on it, or it reads warm.
  • What the heater costs — the 💲 card under the heater thermostat on the Remotes page. Type what you pay for electricity (per kWh, from your bill), the heater's watts, about how many hours a day it's really heating, what you pay for a gallon of propane and your boiler's efficiency (on its label; about 80–85% for an older boiler, 90–95% for a condensing one). It shows what the heater costs per day and per month for different amounts of running, what the boiler would burn to make the same heat, which one is cheaper, and the propane price at which they cost the same. Sentinel can't see when the heater is heating, so the hours are your estimate; the numbers are remembered on each device.
  • Routines — “when this happens, do that”, on the Routines page (or the Routines button on the live page). When: a camera sees a person, vehicle, animal or delivery; any motion on a camera; sunset, sunrise or a set time; someone's phone gets home or leaves; a family car pulls in or drives off. Only (optional): after dark, in daylight, between two times, on certain days, or when nobody's home. Then: turn a switch on (and leave it on, keep it on for some minutes, or until there's been no motion for some minutes) or off, send a Telegram message (with the camera's photo), or say something out loud. Each routine speaks at most every 2 minutes and messages at most every 5, on its own: with one routine per room, walking through the house names each room in turn. Combining Wi-Fi sensors: a sensor routine has three groups, each with its own ＋ Add sensor: any of these starts it, and all of these too must also see movement, but not if any of these must stay still, all within a few seconds of each other (5 unless you change it). “Living room, but not kitchen or dining room” = someone is in the living room, not just walking through. While you edit, a dot on each sensor lights up when it sees movement, a line says whether the routine would go off right now, the routine is read back in plain words, and a 24-hour strip shows when it would have gone off yesterday, so you can tune it before saving. Or just say it: “Hey Jarvis, new routine: when the back yard camera sees someone after dark, turn on the patio lights until there's no motion for 2 minutes.” Claude works it out, Jarvis reads it back, and it's saved when you say “yes” (on Telegram, tap ✅). “What are my routines?” lists them. A light a routine turned on goes off again when its time is up; one someone switched on by hand is left alone. ▶ Try it on the page runs a routine's steps straight away to check it. Routines that only switch lights react to people the camera is less sure about too (someone walking through the yard in the dark), since a light coming on for nothing costs nothing; your alerts and message/speech routines still wait for a clear sighting. “After dark” uses sunrise and sunset for Home on the Family page, or the weather town.
  • Wi-Fi motion sensors — the Sensors page (the Sensors button on the live page). Small ESP32 boards notice people moving by how bodies disturb the Wi-Fi signal between them: every board sends a signal and listens to all the others, so each pair of boards is one line through the house (one card on the page). A line notices movement near the straight path between its two boards, most strongly close to either board, so put boards on opposite sides of the rooms people walk through, about waist to chest high, with no stone or brick wall between them. Sitting still doesn't count as movement, and pets set them off too. Each line learns its quiet room by itself (about half a minute after a board starts or the room settles) and shows Movement or Still with a 2-minute chart. Adjust moves a line's movement line (lower = more sensitive) or relearns its quiet level; Rename names a line after the room it watches; under Boards, Flash light shows which board is which. History shows when each line saw movement, day by day, and you can ask on Telegram or by voice: “Any movement inside today?”, “Anyone in the living room this morning?”. On the Routines page, “a Wi-Fi sensor notices movement” can start a routine. To set a new board up, plug it in: it makes a Wi-Fi network called Sentinel-Sensor-…; join it with your phone and open 192.168.4.1 to pick your home Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz). It appears on the page by itself. Holding its BOOT button for 3 seconds makes it forget the Wi-Fi.
  • House — the House page (🏠 on the home page) is a 3D drawing of your home with your devices in it, live. Drag to turn it, pinch or scroll to zoom, Top looks straight down, and Upstairs / Both show the other floor. Each pair of Wi-Fi sensors is a line between the two boards; it turns yellow while it notices movement, and the room the middle of that line runs through lights up. Switches and TVs glow when they're on; tap one to turn it on or off, tap a camera for its picture. Things worked by the BroadLink (heater, ceiling fans, soundbar: the Remotes page) are purple; tap one for its buttons (the heater also shows what its thermostat is doing). ＋ Add device lists every switch, TV, camera, sensor board and remote that isn't on the map yet: pick one, then tap the floor where it really is. A device you tap can be moved, turned (TVs) or taken off the map, and Device view opens its own page (a camera full screen, a TV's remote, the Sensors or Remotes page). Show ▾ picks what the drawing shows (room names, other labels, device names, sensors, sensor lines, cameras, plugs, TVs, remotes) to make it less busy; each phone or computer remembers its own choice. The drawing itself (rooms, walls, doors, stairs) is made once from a floor plan or photos of your home and saved on this computer only (recordings folder, house.json); Claude Code can redraw it if a room is wrong. On a TV the page starts in the flat Top view.
  • Notes for Claude — say “Jezebel, take a note for Claude”, wait for “Go ahead”, then say what you'd like changed or fixed (pauses are fine; up to about 90 seconds). She saves it and sends you a copy on Telegram. Next time you work with Claude, say “check notes”. You can also say it in one go: “Jezebel, note for Claude: make the music bar bigger.”
  • Music (Spotify) — once Spotify is connected under Spotify: “Hey Jarvis, play some jazz”, “play Tom Petty”, “play Free Fallin'”, “pause” (“pause the music” while the TV is on: plain “pause” is the TV's then), “skip”, “go back”, “volume up / down”, “volume 40”, “what's playing?”, “play on the TV”, “play Spotify on Sentinel” (any speaker's name; if Jezebel doesn't know the name she offers to read out the list — say “yes”). “Play Spotify here” or “on this speaker” means the speaker plugged into this PC (see This PC as a speaker under Spotify). Jarvis learns your Spotify artists (top, followed and recently played, once a day), so band names like Mötley Crüe are heard right; a Spotify connection made before November 2026 needs Connect Spotify once more for that.
  • Hear alerts in a browser — the 🔕 Hear alerts button at the top of the home page and the camera page makes that page read alerts out loud in Jarvis's voice while it's open (turn it on once and it stays on as you move between the two): someone at the door, a car pulling in, a package, a weather warning. The words also show at the top of the page. It's per device: it only talks where you turned it on, and tapping 🔔 Alerts on turns it off again. Browsers only let a page make sound after you've touched it, so after reopening the page the button may say Tap to hear alerts: tap it once. The page doesn't have to be the window you're clicked into, but it has to stay open and showing; a phone with the screen off, or a tab hidden behind another, usually goes quiet (Telegram is the dependable way to get alerts on a phone). It reads the same alerts the house speaker would, chosen under Alerts; not for guests.
  • Talk from your phone — the round 🎙️ button at the bottom right of the home page and the camera page talks to Jarvis through your phone's microphone (or a headset connected to the phone), from anywhere. Tap it, ask as you would in the house, and it stops listening by itself when you stop talking (or tap it again). The answer appears above the button and Jarvis says it on the phone; nothing is said out loud in the house. A question back (“Should I do that?”) shows Yes / No buttons; “take a note for Claude” listens again for the note. The first time, the browser asks to use the microphone: allow it. It needs the https:// address you use away from home (phones only give the microphone to secure pages), and the voice assistant turned on under Audio.
  • Volume — “turn the PC volume down / up” or “PC volume 40” changes everything this PC plays (voice, alerts and music). “Speak up”, “talk quieter” or “set your volume to 60” changes just the voice. “Volume up / down” on its own is the music's (Spotify) volume. The music plays on whichever Spotify device is playing (or the one picked under Spotify); if none is, open Spotify on the TV or your phone first. The bar at the bottom of the live page shows the song and has ⏮ ⏯ ⏭ and volume buttons.
  • Weather — with 3 cameras the grid has a spare space, which shows the weather (set your town or ZIP under Weather). On a phone it's under the cameras. When it's windy it warns you to expect motion clips from trees. Tap it (or press OK on a Fire TV remote) for the full forecast: now in detail (sunrise and sunset under the temperature; wind and its direction, humidity and dew point, UV, pressure, visibility, clouds), the next 48 hours as a chart (hover or tap for any hour) and the next 7 days, with the time as big as on the card. ✕, Esc or Back closes it, and it closes by itself after 2 minutes so a TV goes back to the cameras.
  • Planes overhead — ask Jarvis or Telegram “what plane is that?” (the one nearest overhead) or “any planes around?” (the closest few within about 30 miles): the airline and flight number, the kind of plane, how high it is, which way, and for airline flights where it came from and where it's going. Say “show me the planes” for a map of them on the TV (yellow = below 5,000 feet). Positions come from adsb.lol, a free network of people's home receivers; routes come from adsbdb.com by flight number, and Sentinel only tells a route when the plane is really between those two airports (airlines reuse flight numbers, so a looked-up route is sometimes another leg). Small private planes and helicopters don't share routes, and some military planes don't show at all. “What was that?” right after one goes over looks back about 3 minutes for the plane that passed closest. “Any military planes around?” (or “any C-130s nearby?”) looks out about 170 miles and lists only military aircraft; on the map they're red and labeled “military”. Needs Home saved on the Family page. Away from home (more than about 6 miles), it tells you about the planes over you: the ✈️ Planes tile on the Home page asks your phone for its location (allow it once) and shows the planes around you; after that the 🎙️ Talk button uses it too. On Telegram it goes by your phone's last report (the Family page), matching your Telegram first name to a name or nickname there.
  • ⟳ Reload (top of the live page) — reloads the page, for TVs and browsers without F5. Open pages also reload themselves after Sentinel is updated.
  • Signing in a TV (QR code) — on a big screen the sign-in page shows a QR code. Scan it with your phone's camera (the phone must already be signed in), check the 4-letter code matches, and tap Yes. The TV stays signed in, even after Sentinel restarts. If the phone says the screen is on a different internet connection, only approve if you're standing in front of it.
  • Timeline: on / off (top of the live page, or press T) — hides the timeline so the cameras fill the screen; the grid always arranges the cameras as large as your window allows.

Roles — who can do what
  • Owner — the shared household login. It can do everything, and it alone handles logins (login links, removing a login, making someone an admin) and the household's accounts: the Telegram bot and who gets its messages, adding or removing cameras, Spotify and Claude, remote access and backups. It is always the owner, so it can't be locked out.
  • Admin — a family member's own login that may also change the shared setup: vehicles, people and places, every routine, camera zones, Wi-Fi sensors, remotes and the heater, the house map and alerts. An admin can set others to user or guest, but can't make admins or change another admin.
  • User — uses everything: cameras and recordings, their own timeline (and deleting their own history), switches, TV remotes, the heater, Talk, planes, recipes, notes and mesh chat. They change their own picture and password, and make routines of their own; household routines and other people's are an admin's to change. A user's own routine can't send Telegram messages yet.
  • Guest — live cameras, switches, the lost-dog page, and recordings from the day they became a guest. No family locations.
  • Timeline privacy — a person's timeline is their own. Other users can't open it. The owner and admins see only the last 48 hours of someone else's (enough to help find someone), nothing further back. Pets' history is open to everyone. Where people are right now (the home page, “Where is Liz?”) is unchanged.
  • Notes — the Notes page holds notes and lists. 🔒 Only me (how a new note starts) is shown to nobody else, whatever their role: good for a journal. 👪 The family (how a new list starts) can be seen and changed by everyone: good for the grocery list, where anyone adds or ticks off. Only the writer changes who sees a note; the writer or an admin deletes a shared one. Notes written on the shared household login are always shared. Private notes are never sent to Telegram, Jarvis or Claude; they are kept on the Sentinel PC and in its backup as ordinary text.
  • Set a person's role on the Family page, on their card (Role). A new login starts as a user.
  • Change log (Settings, for the owner and admins) — who changed what in the setup, and when.
  • Jarvis by voice and the Telegram chat aren't covered by roles: anyone in the room, or in the chat, can use them.
Zones and lines
  1. Open Zones and pick a camera.
  2. + Zone: tap each corner of the area to watch, then tap the yellow first point (or Finish zone).
  3. + Line: tap two points. The arrow shows which way someone must cross to trigger; Reverse direction flips it, or set “Lines trigger” to either direction.
  4. Drag the white dots to adjust; double-click a dot to remove it; select a shape and press Delete to remove it.
  5. Select a zone and type a name (e.g. Driveway, Patio) — alerts will use it.
  6. Press Save. Changes apply immediately.

Something counts as motion when the bottom of the moving object (where it touches the ground) is inside a zone, or it crosses a line. No zones and no lines means the whole picture is watched. Keep trees, roads and sky out of zones to cut false alerts.

Sensitivity sets the smallest movement that counts. If leaves or insects trigger clips, lower it; if people are missed, raise it.

Alerts and object detection

Sentinel recognises people, vehicles (car, truck, bus, motorcycle, bicycle) and animals (dog, cat, bird, and larger animals like deer) whenever something moves. With “Recognised objects only” selected under Detection, you get a Telegram photo with a short description — for example “🚗 A red car pulled into the Driveway” or “🚶 Someone is at the Front Door” — followed by the clip if clips are turned on.

  • Leaves, shadows, rain, insects and cloud changes are ignored because they aren't recognised objects.
  • Parked cars don't alert; a vehicle has to move.
  • 📦 Packages: after someone comes to the door, Sentinel compares the porch before and after; something new that looks like a parcel gets a photo in Telegram (“📦 A package was left at the Front Door”), and at sunset a reminder if it's still there. If it disappears while nobody is home, it says so. Cameras with “door” or “porch” in their name are watched. Routines can start on it too (“a camera sees… 📦 Package left”), and can repeat every few hours while it's still there. Tap ✅ Got it under the photo, or tell Jarvis “I got the package”, to stop the reminders. Ask Jarvis “any packages?”.
  • ⚠️ Severe weather: tornado, severe thunderstorm, flash flood and winter storm warnings from the National Weather Service are said out loud as soon as they're issued (day or night), sent to Telegram and shown as a red banner on the camera screens; watches only go to Telegram. Settings > Weather turns them off. Ask Jarvis “any weather alerts?”.
  • 🌅 Morning recap: at 7 am Telegram gets the night in one message (who came by, animals, cars, family cars coming and going, packages waiting, today's weather) and a short video of the moments worth seeing. Send Recap for one any time. Settings > Telegram sets the time.
  • Deliveries: when a vehicle alerts, Sentinel takes a closer look and names UPS, FedEx, Amazon, USPS mail and DHL vans — “📦 An Amazon van pulled in at the Front Door” — and other types like pickup trucks, SUVs and school buses. It looks again as the vehicle comes closer, so a van that stops at the curb may get a second “📦” message. It recognises them by shape and colours, so an unmarked rental van won't be called a delivery, and at night (black-and-white picture) it only names one when it's very sure.
  • Each camera repeats an alert for the same kind of thing at most once per cooldown (60 s by default).
  • Name your zones in Zones so messages say where (“in the Driveway”).
  • Colours are only mentioned in daylight — night (infrared) video is black and white.
Remote access — from anywhere
Telegram commands

Send these to your Sentinel bot (capital letters don't matter):

  • Status — cameras, recording and alert settings.
  • Clip — record about 10 seconds from every camera and send it.
  • Remind me tomorrow at 8am to call the dentist — in plain words; Claude works out the time and you tap ✅ to save it. Or exactly: Remind 2026-11-04 09:00 PT appointment. Reminders lists what's coming up; Cancel reminder 2 removes one.
  • Patio lights on, Turn off the patio lights, Are the patio lights on? — smart plugs and switches, by the names they have on the live page's Switches panel.
  • TV on, Put on Netflix, TV volume up, Mute the TV, Pause the TV, Rewind the TV 30 seconds, What's on the TV? — a Samsung TV on your network.
  • New routine: when a car pulls in after dark, turn on the porch lamp for 5 minutes — in plain words; Sentinel reads it back and you tap ✅ to save. Routines lists them; Routine 2 off / Routine 2 on pauses or restarts one; Delete routine 2.
  • Videos last 2 hours, Videos today, Videos yesterday, Videos 2pm-4pm, Videos since 8am — send recorded clips from that time (up to 8; the ones where something was recognised come first). Add people, vehicles, deliveries or animals and/or a camera name: Videos today people front door, Videos yesterday deliveries.
  • Family cars — on the Vehicles page, name your cars (pick groups of photos Sentinel has taken and say whose car it is). Then alerts use the names (“🛻 Alex's Sierra pulled into the Driveway”) and you can ask When did Elizabeth get home?, Did Ben leave?, Is Kate home? or What cars are in the driveway?. Sentinel looks at the cameras once a minute to see which cars are parked, so times are accurate to about a minute. It recognises the car, not the driver. At night it uses the car's shape; name the 🌙 night photo groups too, and a car it can't place in the dark is named once daylight returns. When a car pulls into the Garage camera's view after dark, Sentinel switches on that camera's white light for about 2½ minutes so it can see the car's real colour (the blue Edge and the grey Fusion look the same in night vision). If a car stays parked a few minutes in daylight and Sentinel can't tell whose it is, Telegram asks “Whose car is this?” with a button for each car — one tap teaches it (at most 3 a day, 8am–9pm; switch it off under Detection).
  • Where's the family? — family members who agree install the free OwnTracks app (the Family page makes a setup link to send them: it asks if they agree, then walks them through installing it and allowing location). Then ask Where is Elizabeth?, Who's home?, Is Mom at work?, When did Ben get to school? or Did Dad leave the gym? — here or to Jezebel. The phone says where the person is and the car is reported separately (people swap cars): “Elizabeth is at the salon. Elizabeth's Fusion is at home.” Telegram also says when someone arrives at or leaves a saved place. To add a place, send a 📍 location pin (attach → Location) and reply with its name, or use the Family page: stand there and pick your phone, or paste coordinates or a Google Maps share link (press and hold the spot first to drop a pin). The Live page's weather card shows a light per person (green = home, amber = out, grey = not sharing); tap a name to open their Family timeline (today, on a map) in the same window, so it works on a TV's kiosk browser too.
  • Pets with a collar tracker — a dog can wear a Meshtastic GPS tracker (for example a SenseCAP T1000-E), heard by a LoRa base station at the house (a Raspberry Pi with a LoRa board; tools/meshtastic_bridge.py passes its positions to Sentinel). On the Family page, add the pet like a person and put the tracker's id (like !a1cacb2a, shown in the Meshtastic app) in Edit; then tap the corners of your yard under Yard. Telegram (with a map pin) and the house speaker say “🐕 Grace left the yard!” within a few metres of the edge, and when she's back. It waits for two readings in a row (about 30 seconds while she's moving), so one stray GPS reading doesn't set it off: a collar left indoors wanders 10–25 m. Ask Where's Grace? (“in the yard” / “outside the yard, about 80 ft north of it”). Say or send The dogs are going out (or Let Grace out, Keep an eye on Grace, or just Dogs outside) and the base asks the collar for its position every 30 seconds for an hour; The dogs are in stops it. Recall Grace or Beep Grace (also beep the dogs) sounds the collar's buzzer within a few seconds, for recall training: in the Meshtastic app set the tracker's Device → Buzzer mode to notifications only and External Notification → Alert bell (buzzer) on, Alert message off, so nothing else makes it beep. Pets don't count as someone being home.
  • Lost dog — the Lost dog tile on the Home page shows a big live map of a collar: where it is now, its trail, the yard, how long ago it was heard and its battery, refreshed every few seconds. Tap 🚨 … is lost: start the search (or say or send “Grace is lost”, “the dogs got out”, “I can't find Grace”): everyone on Telegram gets the news with a map pin and the page's link, the collar is asked for its position every 30 seconds, and a new pin goes out whenever the dog has moved (at most every 5 minutes). The search keeps going through restarts until you tap ✅ Found (or say “we found Grace”). 🧭 Directions opens walking directions in your phone's maps app, 🔔 Beep collar sounds its buzzer. To see yourself on the map too (“You're 300 ft away. Head northwest.”), open Sentinel through its remote web address (https) and allow location. The collar is only heard near the house through the LoRa base, or through other Meshtastic radios in range: if the dog is out of range, the last position on the map is where to start looking. The “left the yard” message includes the live map link as well.
  • Recipes — the family recipe box (🍳 on the Home page). Paste a link to a recipe site and Sentinel keeps just the recipe: ingredients, steps, times, servings and the photo, without the story, ads or videos (most sites carry a tidy copy of the recipe for Google; for the few that don't, Claude reads the page). Instagram reels, TikToks and YouTube Shorts work too: Sentinel downloads the video, listens to it on this PC and Claude writes the recipe up from the caption and what's said; amounts the video never gives are estimated and marked (est.), the video's cover becomes the photo, and ▶ Watch the reel opens the original. A video takes a minute or so; you can leave the page. If a site won't open (or a reel needs a login), paste the recipe text instead. You can also send a link to the Telegram bot, or on an Android phone tap Share → Sentinel (once Sentinel is installed to the home screen). On a recipe: change the servings and every amount follows; Swap next to an ingredient asks Claude what to use instead (with the amount, and any step it changes); Give it your own twist asks Claude to change the whole recipe (“spicier”, “dairy-free”); Edit recipe changes anything by hand and adds notes. Changes show in amber until you tap Save as our version; everyone then sees your version, and Original shows the recipe as it was. Add your own photo, pick a category (Breakfast, Dinners, Sides, Snacks, Desserts, Beverages), and search the box by name or ingredient. What can I make? ranks the box by what you have (also by voice or Telegram: What can I make with chicken and rice?). Start cooking shows one step at a time in big text with a timer button wherever a step says how long; Jarvis follows along: Next step, Go back a step, How much garlic?, Read the ingredients, Start the timer or Set a timer for 10 minutes (he says when it's done), How much longer?. Swaps, twists, videos and pages without recipe data use Claude (Settings → Assistant), counted in its daily limit. Recipes are shared by everyone signed in; they are not private.
  • Mesh chat — the LoRa base station that hears the collar can also text family and friends over the Meshtastic radio mesh, with no phone service or internet. Open Mesh chat (📡 on the Home page). Invite someone who has a Meshtastic radio and the app: they scan a code with their phone's camera. Chat one-to-one adds the base station to their contacts so they can message it straight away; direct messages are private once the two radios have swapped keys (🔒). Family group chat is a private channel (tap Add the Family channel once): one message reaches everyone who joined; anyone with that code can read it, so share it only with family. Radios often have call signs or odd names: tap ✏️ next to one to give it a nickname (“Cousin Dave”) that only you see, everywhere on the page and in Telegram. Pick a chat, type and press Send (one radio message holds about 200 letters); under each message it says sent, a radio passed it on, delivered or why it didn't go. New direct and Family messages come to Telegram (📡 Name (mesh): …), Jarvis says “You have a mesh message from Cousin Dave” (not between 10 pm and 7 am, and once per person every couple of minutes), and the Live page shows a glowing 📡 2 at the end of the family lights until you read them; the public channel's chatter does none of that. Say Read my mesh messages (or Any mesh messages?, also on Telegram) and Jarvis reads them out, oldest first, and marks them read. Turn Telegram or Jarvis off under Base station. There you can also rename the base station: everyone on the mesh sees the name, so use a nickname, not your address. Messages wait while the base station is offline and give up after 10 minutes. The base station needs the current tools/meshtastic_bridge.py.
  • Family timeline — like Google Maps Timeline: the Family timeline page has a calendar for each person; tap a day to see where they stopped (and for how long), the drives between, and the day on a map. A stop that isn't a saved place shows how far it is from one, with a Name this place button. Everyone who signs in can see everyone's timeline. Recordings and storage

Each clip is recorded twice: a small copy from the camera's substream, and — when the camera's main stream is available — a sharp high-resolution copy that starts 5 seconds before the motion (kept in a short rolling buffer in memory). Playback and Telegram use the high-res copy when there is one (shown as “HD” in the player).

Sentinel records a clip when motion is detected and keeps clips for the number of days set under Recording (30 by default). If the disk gets nearly full it deletes the oldest clips first, but never the last 24 hours. Clips are stored on the PC in recorded_videos/, one folder per day.

Backup

Every night (3:30 am by default) Sentinel copies its databases, settings, family and car photos, recipes and the last few days of video to a USB drive plugged into this PC. A 128 GB USB 3 thumb drive holds everything plus about two weeks of video. The databases are copied safely while Sentinel keeps running, the last 7 nights are kept, and the oldest video on the drive makes room for the newest. If a backup goes wrong, or the drive gets unplugged, you get a Telegram message.

Setting up a drive: plug it in, open Backup and tap Use this drive for backups. Its other files are left alone: the backup goes in a Sentinel Backup folder, with a README.txt that explains how to restore.

One-time permission: Sentinel runs in the background, so Linux doesn't let it open USB drives until you allow it. If the Backup section says so, run this once in a terminal on the PC (it asks for your password):

echo 'polkit.addRule(function(a, s) { if (s.user == "'$USER'" && (a.id == "org.freedesktop.udisks2.filesystem-mount" || a.id == "org.freedesktop.udisks2.filesystem-mount-other-seat")) return polkit.Result.YES; });' | sudo tee /etc/polkit-1/rules.d/50-sentinel-usb.rules

If a drive still won't open after that, the PC's disk service may be stuck: sudo systemctl restart udisks2 fixes it (it doesn't touch any data).

Another computer: to keep a copy away from this PC too (in case of fire or theft), type the SSH name of another computer on your network, such as a Raspberry Pi, under Backup. This PC needs to be able to sign in to it with an SSH key, and it gets the databases, settings and photos (no video).

Troubleshooting
  • A camera shows offline — check it has power and network, then press Reconnect under Cameras. Sentinel also retries on its own.
  • Too many alerts — draw zones that leave out trees and the street, lower sensitivity, or raise the minimum seconds between alerts.
  • No alerts on Telegram — check “Telegram bot on” and “Send motion alerts”, then press Send test message. Make sure the chat isn't paused (send Resume).
  • A clip won't play — it may have been deleted by retention; otherwise reload the page.
  • Logged out unexpectedly — sessions end after an hour of inactivity and when Sentinel restarts.
  • Remote Desktop shows a black screen — log out of the desktop at the PC itself (locking is not enough), then connect again.
